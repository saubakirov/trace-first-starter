## 4. Recommendations (suggestions, not blocking)

1. Keep a short transitional-consumer statement beside each new operational contract so later phases cannot mistake a canonical definition for already-implemented workflow behavior.
2. Use compact ownership and scenario tables in `conventions.md` where they distinguish behavior; avoid repeating philosophy or glossary definitions there.
3. Treat the eight numeric objects as a restore-owner-or-retire decision queue. Record no replacement values and do not reinterpret observed breaches as calibration evidence.
4. Preserve the current research procedure as a comparative decision method while avoiding any implication that `focused` or `deep` changes inquiry logic.

## 5. Risks Found (edge cases, potential issues not in TS)

1. **Definition/contract duplication:** the same term can easily acquire a concise glossary definition, a formal conventions contract, and a third quasi-definition in a README. The ownership scan must distinguish summaries and references from competing definitions.
2. **Transitional contradiction:** current workflows still encode the older inline/reference and evidence contracts. Phase A must identify them as later consumers without changing or falsely declaring them compliant.
3. **Proof terminology collision:** `Verification`, `Evidence`, `Proof Record`, and `Value Debt` must remain distinct enough that later phases can map existing artifacts without collapsing synthetic checks into real-world observation.
4. **Numeric ledger overreach:** assigning a provisional class can be misread as validating the current number or authorizing removal. Every row needs an explicit transitional disposition and owner decision.
5. **Scope spill:** broad synonym cleanup may expose valid downstream consumers, but Phase A may modify only the four canonical files plus its TFW traces. Out-of-scope findings must be reported rather than fixed.
6. **Working-tree provenance:** the coordinator's TFW-48 planning artifacts and Task Board entry were present as uncommitted work at onboarding. Executor changes must preserve them exactly and keep commits scoped and auditable.
