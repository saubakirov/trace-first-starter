1. **AC-1 was run first, and it cleared — no thinning, no stop.** The honest draft measured **753** words
   against 483 (+56 %), so the compression was real work rather than a formality. The 480 that shipped was
   reached by eight named subtractions, every one of them terminology or duplication, listed with its
   justification in `evidence/word_budget.md`. **No mechanism was thinned**, which is the only thing DoF 2
   forbids. Research's 499-vs-483 figure did not apply: it described a superseded mechanism set, and the
   figure that decided the AC was re-measured against the text actually written.

2. **The mechanism is in `review.md`; its canonical home is `conventions.md` §5 — and the line is made
   checkable rather than promised.** §5 grew 952 → 1 433 words. AC-1's added bullet says the reason may
   live there and the mechanism may not, so the RF states the test a reader can run: delete §5's new
   subsection and `review.md` still instructs the reviewer to locate, test, route, propose and count — it
   just stops saying where a routed item lands. Delete `review.md`'s additions and the mechanism has no
   site at all. §5 is also the only legal home: every verdict route already lives there and DoF 1 forbids
   creating a file.

3. **Step 5 became a table, and that is a compression decision with a second benefit.** The five acts —
   Filter, Axis, Test, Route, Propose — cost 55 fewer words as a two-column table than as prose, and the
   sequence reads as structure rather than as a paragraph a reader has to hold in their head.

4. **Pattern A, per the coordinator's ruling on ONB Q3.** `review.md` Step 4 carries
   `Max revision cycles | 2 | Hard | tfw.review.max_revision_cycles` — the number and the key together, in
   the same table shape Step 2 uses for `min_verify_ratio` twelve lines above. An agent that cannot reach
   the config still has the number, and §11's *"Pure refs (Pattern B) = broken"* is not breached in the
   one file that already obeys it.

5. **The return is a `transition` to `❌ BLOCKED`, per the coordinator's ruling on ONB Q4.** No kind was
   invented. `conventions.md` §5 already defines `transition` as recording *"lifecycle changed, blockage
   and resumption included"*, and `BLOCKED` as waiting on a dependency that clears — an owner ruling that
   has not arrived is one. `dispatch` was refused because it records a handover and the load-bearing fact

### 8.2 — round 2: strategic insights (execution)

1. **The defect was a missing artifact, not a missing rule — and that is the generalisable finding.** Round
   1 built *the reviewer proposes, the coordinator rules* and built it correctly, for dispositions. It did
   not build it for the round, because the coordinator had **no artifact of its own in which a round
   exists**. Every symptom followed from that one absence: the executor needed a detector, `status.md`
   could not say a round was open, the round was invisible in a directory listing, and the rest lived in
   chat. Four patches were proposed for four symptoms. Naming the artifact removed all four and added
   nothing. **The test for the next task of this shape:** when a role must write a fact, ask which file it
   owns that can hold it, before asking what the fact should say.
2. **A rule can be refuted by trying to obey it, and that is cheaper than reviewing it.** Three of the
   order's nine items were wrong, and all three surfaced in the twenty minutes an executor spent reading
   the files the order names — not in the review that approved the order, and not in the coordinator's own
   re-read. The ONB is where that happens, and it earned its cost on this round more clearly than on any
   round in this corpus: five questions, three corrections to the contract, no count spent.
3. **Withdrawing a shipped mechanism is cheaper than the mechanism, and only inside an uncut version.**
   Fourteen references in twelve tracked files went to zero in one commit series, with no migration step
   and nothing for a receiver to remove, **because 2.1.0 was never tagged**. The same withdrawal one tag
   later would have owed every receiving project a deletion instruction, a searchable string in the canon,
   and an allowlist entry that outlives the mechanism. The window in which a wrong decision is free to
   reverse closes at the tag, not at the commit.
4. **A budget and a bar answer the same question and produce different failures.** A count fails by
   arithmetic: it fires on the legitimate case, and it fired on this task's own within hours. A citation
   bar fails by judgement: nothing structural stops a reviewer citing the same criterion twice on two bad
   fixes, and TS §8 records that as the residual. The trade is a mechanism that misfires predictably for
   one that depends on the coordinator writing an honest basis cell — and the basis cell is at least
   readable by someone who was not there, which the count never was.
5. **`conventions.md` §5 is now 1 673 words and has taken +721 across two rounds** on a section that
   started at 952. It is not budgeted, and that is the whole reason it grew: DoD 10 put a gate on
   `review.md` and the mechanism landed next door. The compression task inherits a measured number rather
   than a complaint — and the lesson for its contract is that a word gate on one file relocates growth
   rather than preventing it.

### 8.3 — round 3: strategic insights (execution)

1. **The smallest fix is a bounded question, and asking it is cheaper than the round it saves.** Three
   questions cost one exchange. Left unasked, each produces a fourth round: two stale glossary references
   are a citable defect, four undocumented shipped changes are a DoD 13 breach, and an unasserted regex
   exception is the order's own failure condition with no detector. **The generalisable form:** when an
   order names a repair, ask what reads the thing being repaired. Item 2's readers were two glossary
   entries, item 5's reader is `update.md` Step 4, and item 1's reader is the next person to touch the
   regex.
2. **The citation bar's prediction was tested this round and it held — for six items and not for the
   seventh thing.** TS revision 3 §1 enumerated the citable set and claimed it would be empty afterwards.
   Six items closed it. What was not in the enumeration is a defect the ordering commit itself introduced,
   which is a **new citation** and therefore a legitimate fourth round by the bar's own terms. That is the
   bar working: the round is reachable because a condition can be named, not because anyone wants one.
3. **An immutable artifact makes a small defect expensive, and that is the price of the guarantee.** Three
   code points over a ceiling would be a one-character fix in any mutable file. In a journal event it is a
   decision about whether immutability admits a repair, and every available answer costs something —
   editing the record, leaving a malformed file the gate keeps reading, or moving a limit to fit a
   violation. **Worth stating for whoever writes the rule:** immutability needs a stated procedure for a
   malformed event, and this corpus does not have one. `conventions.md` §5 already carries the pattern for
   the analogous case — `UNDECLARED` in `status.md`, where a tool may never resolve it and an accountable
   owner may, leaving an event as the trace.
4. **Two rounds running, the thing that found the defect was an executor obeying an order literally.**
   Round 2 found step 7 by deviating from it on purpose and having to justify that. Round 3 found the stale
   before figure by measuring instead of quoting, and the ambiguous renumbering by an edit that refused to
   run on two matches. None of the three came from review, re-reading, or a checklist. **The mechanism is
   that obedience is a test:** a rule you must follow exactly is a rule whose contradictions surface at the
   keystroke.
5. **`handoff.md` fell by three words and the compression task inherits 1 727 against a 1 200 ceiling.**
   Round 3 is the first round in this task to make a workflow shorter by deleting a step rather than by
   compressing prose, and the yield was three words — because the step that went was replaced by an
   equivalent step elsewhere. The lesson for `TFW_20260902-175227_RCFR`: relocating an instruction is not
   subtraction, and a file 44 % over its ceiling does not get there by moving steps around.

### 8.4 — round 4: strategic insights (execution)

**The defect pattern this task kept hitting has a shape, and round 4 is the first round that did not add
to it.** Rounds 1, 2 and 3 each answered a correct catch with the smallest change that satisfied the
sentence, and each left the job half done in the same way: the sentence was satisfied and the *purpose*
behind it was not. Round 3's two assertions are the cleanest instance in the corpus — written to make a
widening detectable, and both aimed where a widening cannot occur. **What breaks the pattern is not more
care; it is a procedure that produces evidence the care worked.** For a guard, that procedure is: make the
guard fail on purpose before believing it passes. It costs one command, and it is the only thing that
distinguishes enforcement from advice.

**A derived view in a blocking gate is a category error, and the corpus now has the worked example.** The
canon already said the board *is never authoritative* and that its absence *changes no task state*, and
the same project wired its checker into `build.verify`. One over-long summary in one unrelated task then
stopped every test in the repository, and there was no compliant repair inside any task's contract,
because the artifact holding the violation is immutable by rule. **The instructive part is which of the
two things moved.** The tempting repairs all touch the rule — edit the event, raise the ceiling — and each
is the instrument A13 rejected by name. The repair taken removes the *position* the check held and leaves
the check, the rule and the violation exactly as they were. `gen_index.py --check tasks` still exits 1
today. **Nothing was relaxed; something was moved out of a place it should never have been.** The general
test an executor can apply before letting a check block: ask whether the thing it reads is authoritative.
If the canon says it is not, the check is a report.

**Reporting a gate with its survivor in it is worth more than a clean gate.** Item 2's gate does not pass
as written — `glossary.md` still holds one live `handoff.md` step number. It would have been trivial to
quote a narrower `grep` that returns nothing, and every subsequent reader would have believed the file was
clean. Instead the survivor is printed, classified on three grounds, and handed to the reviewer as
overturnable. **A gate whose output has been shaped to pass has stopped being a gate**, and the corpus
cannot tell the difference afterwards — which is precisely the failure mode
`docs/scripts/test_integration.py` carries a docstring about.

### 8.5 — round 5: strategic insights (execution)

**The cheapest false statement in a project is a number, and a release entry is the worst place to keep
one.** Three of this entry's counts went stale inside a single evening, and none of them was load-bearing:
each decorated a fact that was already true and already re-derivable. The instructive part is the
asymmetry. A figure costs one word to write and creates an obligation that lasts as long as the file — and
a CHANGELOG entry, once tagged, is never rewritten in substance, so the obligation outlives everyone who
could discharge it. That is why the coordinator's *prefer removing to correcting* is not a shortcut but
the stronger repair: correcting a figure fixes today's instance and renews the obligation, while removing
it ends the class. **The one question that decides each case: does the sentence still make its point
without the number?**

**A gate on a claim's *truth* is not a gate on its *derivability*, and §7.1 asks for both.** The review
found the truth half failing and the order paid it. The half that would have gone unpaid is the one the
placeholder exposed: `evidence/round4.md` recorded its measurement as `$ python  #` — a command shaped
like a command, with no way to run it. A reader could neither reproduce the figure nor discover that it
had drifted, which is precisely how 116 survived a round of review that verified four files at 100%. **A
figure with no runnable command is unfalsifiable, and an unfalsifiable claim in an evidence file is worth
less than no claim**, because it looks like proof. Writing the command is what turned this round's own
figures into things I could check against my own draft — and two of them failed that check.

**Checking beat assuming twice in a two-item round, and both times the instinct was to add.** Item 2 looked
incomplete as well as false: `update.md` and `research/base.md` also open with a `## Step 0`, so the
obvious move was to cite four workflows instead of two. Reading those two steps showed they are *pin the
source* and *Resume & Iteration Detection* — the same number, a different convention. Had I trusted the
grep, the repair for a false universal would have been a *wider* false universal. The same reflex nearly
put *four of eleven workflows* into the entry, which would have introduced exactly the maintained count
item 1 exists to remove. **In a round whose subject is false claims, the executor's own additions deserve
the same suspicion as the text under repair.**
