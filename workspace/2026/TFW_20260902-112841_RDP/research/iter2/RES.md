## Conclusion

This iteration replayed a real rung-2 finding through the route the coordinator applied ninety minutes
before it started, and the route survived the part that matters and failed the part that was invented.
**It is right that the coordinator is the decider for an item the TS cannot accept** — a within-task
control in `ai-first-devices` shows the same reviewer, in the same week, getting four rounds when the item
went to the executor's list and three rounds ending in APPROVE when it went to the coordinator by name.
**It is wrong that the delivery mechanism is a lifecycle value**: `lifecycle` is one field per task and a
rung is a property per item, so the ordinary mixed-rung round has no representable state. H3 came out
better than the coordinator's entry prepared for — the site exists, it was shipped by TLD three days ago,
and it is the disposition cell together with its `pending — {role}` state, in which **two of three
outcomes cannot be stamped because TLD made a disposition name an artifact that must exist.** The owner is
not forced to choose between DoF 1 and the authority move.

**What research provided that would have been missed.** The termination rule's escape hatch. HL §10 states
that the round is *"already visible in the artifact names — `rev2`, `rev3` — and that is the structural
home"*; `innoforce-ai-first` has been running **nine revisions of one deliverable under nine surface names,
with three reviews and no terminations**, and it is doing so on deliverable 5's own instruction that work
the existing TS cannot accept takes a new TS. Neither the round count nor A2's approved trigger crosses a
TS boundary. Without this iteration the task would have shipped a termination rule whose defeat costs a
filename, against a DoF item that names exactly that failure — and the corpus that demonstrates it was
counted as having zero deep rounds because iteration 1's grep assumed where the round marker lives.

**Self-critique.** Three weaknesses, and the first is the one that should be read. **(1)** The replay is on
paper. It establishes that a mixed-rung round has no representable state — which is a reading of two closed
schemas and is as strong as reading gets — but every claim about what the repaired route *would* feel like
is inference from one corpus arm that never had a `status.md` at all. AFD evidences the **landing site**;
it evidences nothing about the **lifecycle move**, and iteration 1's A3 fused the two. **(2)** H3's site is
confirmed with a residual I could not close: nothing fails when a `not material` consequence is generic,
and this corpus has already produced nine such rulings in a row. I state H3 confirmed because two of three
outcomes are structurally unstampable, not because the third is safe. **(3)** E4 and C5 argue against a
change this researcher proposed and the coordinator applied this morning; I found it only because a
re-count I ran out of discipline turned up four files, and I would not have looked otherwise. The
discipline was iteration 1's D3, which is the reason it caught its own author.
