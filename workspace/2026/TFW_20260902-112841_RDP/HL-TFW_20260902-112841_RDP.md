## 1. Vision 🔒 FROZEN

A review produces two kinds of output — work ordered now, and work not done — and both become decisions by
a stated rule. One question decides whether a finding deserves work, and it is the project's own question:
*does leaving this undone damage purpose, inspectability, authority or continuation?* One role rules on
each kind of output, named before the autonomy is granted. And the loop ends in a decision rather than in
exhaustion: a third round is not a third attempt, it is the signal that the specification was wrong.

**Impact:** A reviewer stops inventing a private standard for each finding, and a coordinator stops
discovering at release time that the loop never closed. The word *revision* exists in the canon with a
meaning. A returning executor has a door to come back through. And `review.md` is shorter than it is
today while carrying all of this — because what was missing was terms, not paragraphs.

> "I stopped arguing about whether a finding matters and started answering one question about it. The
> loop closed twice and stopped, and the third time it stopped us instead."

### 2.5 The predecessor already did the design work

`PROPOSAL__TFW-58__revise_protocol` — 2026-08-13, `lifecycle: TODO`, `owner: unassigned`, in the frozen
legacy corpus. Its measurements were re-verified on 2026-09-02 and **not one has moved.** Its central
insight is that the owner's question is malformed:

> The question *"coordinator in the loop or not?"* conflates **who decides what gets fixed** with **who
> does the fixing**.

Separate them and a ladder falls out of mechanisms already ruled on:

```text
reviewer files a REVISE finding
      │
      ├─ satisfiable inside the approved TS?
      │     YES → executor fixes, reviewer re-checks.   COORDINATOR NOT NEEDED
      │            the majority case, and the owner's own shortcut — legitimate, not a corner cut
      │
      │     NO  → the TS must change
      │            → COORDINATOR, because only the coordinator may change a TS;
      │              an executor doing it is a Role Lock violation
      │
      └─ unsatisfiable without touching a frozen section?
             → OWNER, through the amendment protocol
```

**The ladder was field-validated before it was written down.** On TFW-60 the reviewer independently reached
the route the sketch prescribes, twice in one day — *"the narrowest viable route is `TS_DRAFT`"* on a
REJECT, and *"does not require HL or TS rework"* on a REVISE. Same shape as refinement / amendment /
verdict: not an invention, the transfer of a ruled mechanism onto a second surface.

It also answers *revision or new work* without appeal to taste:

> **If a finding needs a TS change, it is not a revision — it is new work wearing a revision's name.**

### 2.7 The reviewer was given authority nobody granted

`review.md` Step 5 currently has the **reviewer** rule dispositions. `conventions.md` §15 gives the
reviewer *"review stage files, REVIEW"* and forbids the rest; nothing there grants acceptance authority
over whether new work exists. The coordinator authored that step — this coordinator — and it was a design
error, not a detail.

The distinction that resolves it: a **REVISE finding** orders work inside boundaries the owner already
approved. A **disposition** decides whether new work exists at all. The first is bounded by the approved
TS and can be routed by rule; the second is acceptance authority and belongs to the coordinator.

Owner ruling, 2026-09-02: *«ревьювер может разметить, но не решить»* — the reviewer marks up, the
coordinator decides. Scoped by the coordinator to **one act at the close of review**, not one act per item,
on NS2 principle 6.

## 4. Phases 🔒 FROZEN

**Single phase, and deliberately no more.** The three mechanisms are one release surface: they land in
`review.md`, `handoff.md`, `conventions.md` §5 and §15, and the review templates — the same files, in the
same pass, in the same version.

**The owner ruled out a reserve phase on 2026-09-02, on grounds worth recording:** *«не будем себе стелить
соломку, это даст возможность агентам как бы расслабиться и не учесть ценности сразу»* — a phase held in
reserve is permission to defer accounting for values, and an agent that has a later round will use it. The
coordinator had argued the structural case for the same conclusion; the owner's reason is the stronger one
and is the one on record.

**Declared outcome:** every finding a review produces carries one stated basis, one named decider and one
terminating rule; a returning executor has an entry point; the artifacts of a revision round are named as
appended, sibling or untouched; and `review.md` ends at or under its own budget with all of it added.

**Deliverables:**

1. **The criterion**, stated once, derived from `NS1` and cited to it: *does leaving this undone damage
   purpose, inspectability, authority or continuation?* It replaces the contradiction in §2.4 — the
   entry filter tests whether an observation is real, and materiality is decided once, at disposition.
2. **`not material` states a prediction, not a priority.** The ruling names what will not happen, so it
   can be wrong later and be seen to be wrong. *"Low priority"* cannot be checked and is not admissible.
3. **The routing ladder** for ordered work, in `review.md` and mirrored in `conventions.md` §5:
   TS-satisfiable → executor; needs a TS change → coordinator; needs a frozen change → owner.
4. **Disposition authority moves to the coordinator** — one act at the close of review, not one per item.
   `review.md` Step 5 has the reviewer mark and propose; `conventions.md` §15 names acceptance authority
   so the grant is visible where the role is defined.
5. **A revision is defined**, in the canon, using the word: repair of what was already specified. Work
   that cannot be accepted under the existing TS is new work and takes a new TS.
6. **Termination:** two rounds, then the verdict must end it — accept and name what remains, or reject and
   return to planning. Stated with its consequence, not as advice.
7. **A re-entry point in `handoff.md`** for an executor returning after a REVISE: what to read, what not
   to re-do, and what the round is bounded by.
8. **Revision artifacts named:** which artifact is appended to, which gets a sibling, which is never
   overwritten. The RF is the case that decides the rule — it is the highest-authority artifact and it is
   the one that was destroyed.
9. **Every section this task edits ends no larger than it started**, with every mechanism above included,
   measured per section before and after. Sections not touched are not touched, and the project's own
   ≤1 200 ceiling is neither met nor amended here — §2.8 shows the cuttable mass sits outside this task's
   authority, and re-deriving the number belongs to the compression task.
10. **All three adapter sets and both marker blocks re-synced**, per `conventions.md` §9.
11. **Lands in 2.1.0**, which is written and not yet tagged. No separate release; the CHANGELOG's existing
    entry gains this work and the tag is cut when both tasks are in.

## 6. Definition of Failure (DoF) 🔒 FROZEN

- ❌ 1. Anything new that must be maintained: a file, template, script, check or configuration key.
- ❌ 2. Any section this task edits ends larger than it started. The per-section budget is the proof that
  terms replaced paragraphs; missing it means the opposite happened. **Equally a failure: reaching the
  budget by cutting a section this task was not authorised to touch**, or by amending `conventions.md`
  §11's ceiling to accommodate this file.
- ❌ 3. The criterion is stated but nothing can fail against it — NS2's own test: *"Each principle must
  change a work choice. If it only decorates a document, it is not functioning as a principle."*
- ❌ 4. The termination rule has no defined consequence, so a third round remains reachable by anyone
  willing to write one.
- ❌ 5. The reviewer retains disposition authority anywhere in the canon, or the coordinator's grant is
  stated in a workflow but not where the role is defined.
- ❌ 6. Routing is made uniform for simplicity — every finding through the coordinator — which taxes the
  majority case to fix the minority one and contradicts NS2 principle 6.
- ❌ 7. Deferral reappears under a new name: any outcome that accepts an item without a decision.
- ❌ 8. A rule is added to the workflows this task does not touch, or a section it does not touch is
  rewritten. Compression of the other workflows belongs to its own task.
- ❌ 9. The scope budget is exceeded without stopping and reporting first.

**On failure:** stop, report the measurement that failed, and route by the ladder this task is defining —
if the fix needs no contract change it returns to execution; if it does, it comes back here.

## 7. Principles 🔒 FROZEN

1. **The criterion is NS1's, not ours.** The test for whether work deserves doing is quoted from the north
   star and cited to it. A locally invented standard is what §2.4 already produced eight times.
2. **Authority is named before autonomy is granted.** NS2 principle 4. A role that rules on something the
   role definition does not mention is a grant nobody made.
3. **Proportional routing.** NS2 principle 6. The majority case pays nothing; only a change of
   specification or of contract costs an escalation.
4. **A loop ends in a decision, never in exhaustion.** NS2 principle 5 — finish with an inspectable result
   and an authoritative next step or an explicit close.
5. **A ruling states a prediction.** A refusal that cannot later be shown wrong is not a ruling, it is a
   preference. This is what makes `not material` honest rather than cheap.
6. **Subtraction is the proof.** Three mechanisms arrive and the file gets shorter. Where a paragraph is
   standing in for a missing term, name the term and delete the paragraph — bloat is a terminology failure,
   not a word count.
7. **A rule with no enforcement site is decoration.** `knowledge/process.md` F30. Where a rule can be
   structural — an artifact that must exist, a column that must be filled — it is structural, not an
   exhortation.

## 10. RESEARCH Case 🟢 FREE

### Blind Spots

- Whether a two-round cap is the right number, or whether the corpus supports a different one. Three
  surfaces exceeded two rounds; that is a small sample to legislate from.
- What other projects already invented for their own revise loops, and — sharper — **why `helpdesk` barely
  loops at all: 71 REVIEW files, one repeat round.** `ai-first-devices` is the opposite pole with 150
  REVIEW files across 46 tasks on TFW 0.9.0, review templates byte-identical to upstream. Neither corpus
  has ever been read for this question.
- Whether the criterion, applied to the 85 existing REVIEW files' findings, discriminates at all — or
  whether it admits everything and therefore decides nothing.

### Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | The criterion discriminates: applied to real findings it produces a materially different split from the eight ad-hoc rulings in §2.4, and at least one of those eight would change | open |
| H2 | Two rounds is the right cap: in the corpus, rounds beyond the second correct wording or specification rather than work — so the cap would have forced an earlier honest verdict, not lost a fix | open |
| H3 | The coordinator's single act can be made non-rubber-stamp without a new artifact — the mechanism exists in text already written (a verdict line, a column, an existing event kind) rather than needing a new one | open |
| H4 | The three mechanisms fit under 1 200 words in `review.md` — the file's excess is prose standing in for terms, per F40, and naming the terms recovers more than 500 words | open |
| H5 | The loops invented elsewhere converge on the same ladder the sketch prescribes, as TFW-60's reviewer independently did. `ai-first-devices` is the strong test at one pole — 150 REVIEW files, revisions inside one artifact, 18 sub-phases of which an unknown share are revision rounds wearing a phase name | open |
| H6 | **`helpdesk` avoids the loop for a locatable reason, not by luck** — 71 REVIEW files, one repeat round. Either its reviews are less demanding, or something in its practice resolves findings before a second round is needed. If the second, that mechanism is worth more than the cap | open |

> **Filter:** H1 false → the criterion is decoration and DoF 3 fires before the work starts. H2 false → the
> cap is arbitrary and must be re-derived or dropped. H3 false → the authority move needs a new entity,
> which DoF 1 forbids, so the design changes shape. H4 false → DoD 10 and the deliverables cannot both
> hold, and the owner must rule which gives. H5 false → the ladder is this repository's habit rather than a
> general mechanism, and the receiving prose must be written far more carefully. H6 is the one hypothesis
> whose confirmation could *replace* part of the design rather than support it: a practice that removes the
> need for a second round beats a rule that forbids a third.

### Risks of Not Researching

We would legislate a round cap from three observations, and ship a criterion nobody has tested against a
real finding. H1 and H4 are the two that can fail the task outright, and both are answerable by
measurement rather than by opinion.

### Proposed RESEARCH Focus

1. **Gather — three corpora, three different behaviours, and the contrast is the finding.** Measured
   2026-09-02:

   | Corpus | REVIEW files | Repeat rounds | Practice |
   |---|---:|---:|---|
   | `ai-first-devices` (0.9.0) | 150 across 46 tasks | deep; 18 numbered sub-phases | revisions inside one artifact |
   | this repository (2.1.0) | 85 | 8 surfaces, up to 4 rounds | a second full phase |
   | `helpdesk` (2.0.0-dirty.5) | 71 | **1** | **almost never loops** |

   **`helpdesk` is the control case, and the owner named it for research on 2026-09-02.** A corpus of 71
   reviews with a single repeat round is the most useful object here: *what does it do that removes the
   need to loop?* That is worth more than another example of deep looping. Then `innoforce-ai-first`,
   `kaznpu-ai-lab` and `KZ-IT-telegram-list` for coverage. Read-only throughout; no receiving tree is
   modified, per the boundary TLD established.
2. **Extract (H2)**: every round beyond the second in this corpus — four files. What did each round
   actually correct: work, wording, or specification? This is the cap's whole evidence base.
3. **Extract (H1)**: the criterion applied to a sample of real findings from existing REVIEW files, scored
   blind against how they were actually dispositioned.
4. **Challenge**: argue that the criterion is unfalsifiable in practice — that *purpose, inspectability,
   authority, continuation* can be argued for any finding — and find the strongest case that it decides
   nothing.

### Why Not Just…?

- **Why not put the criterion in the template and leave the workflow alone?** A template carries format;
  the decision happens in the workflow, and F30 says capture without an enforcement site changes nothing.
- **Why not count rounds in a control file?** It is a new maintained artifact, which DoF 1 forbids. The
  round is already visible in the artifact names — `rev2`, `rev3` — and that is the structural home.
- **Why not route every finding through the coordinator?** DoF 6. It taxes the majority case to fix the
  minority one, and the owner's own practice is the counter-evidence: he took the shortcut for months and
  the ladder says he was right to.
- **Why not raise the word ceiling instead of meeting it?** The ceiling would then have moved twice to
  accommodate the files that breached it, which is how a number stops being a constraint. Re-deriving it
  from the corpus is a legitimate question and it belongs to the compression task, with evidence.
- **Why not leave the reviewer ruling dispositions, since the first use went well?** One reviewer, nine
  rows, same session lineage as the executor. It proves the mechanism can produce reasoned refusals, not
  that the authority was granted.
