## 1. Vision 🔒 FROZEN

A team uses one named computer for sustained AI work across several projects and pursues one shared paid subscription where the selected provider's terms permit it. A person can prepare an HL on a personal computer, publish it, and another person can start authorized work on the common computer. The resulting trace distinguishes the HL author, declared launch initiator, accountable owner, initiating device when observable, execution host, exact working unit, project/task and mandate without presenting a self-declaration as authenticated identity.

**Impact:** The team can pool computation without losing the ability to reconstruct which person claimed a launch, which computer performed the work, and which authority allowed it. Subscription savings are a target to verify, not a current fact.

> **Explicitly imagined stakeholder quote:** “I can see that Bek declared the launch of Anna's approved project on TEAM-01, while Dana's other project ran separately; the record tells me what was observed and what was merely declared.”

## 3. Target State (To-Be) 🔒 FROZEN

The Full TFW reference defines a small, provider-neutral provenance contract for personal and shared computers. A common host has a stable declared name; TEAM-01 is the proposed pilot label for the observed machine, subject to owner approval with this HL. Its OS hostname and network address are observations, not the TFW host identity. The launching person is recorded separately with the source and strength of that claim. Each authority-bearing launch and material durable act links project/task/phase, exact unit and immutable mandate. A personal client device and a remote execution host are distinct fields when both can be observed; an unavailable field remains explicitly unknown. Parallel projects keep separate workspaces, sessions, access and traces. A receiving scenario proves the contract on the owner's common computer and reports whether the selected paid subscription may be used by multiple people under its actual terms.

The contract records significant starts and durable acts. It does not demand a log of every click, model token or transient internal step. Transport remains a selected means of access, not the source of TFW authority.

## 5. Definition of Done (DoD) 🔒 FROZEN

- ✅ 1. A reader can distinguish HL author, declared initiator, accountable owner, principal when relevant, personal initiating device when observed, stable execution-host name, exact working unit, project/task and mandate. Unknown and self-declared facts are labeled by evidence strength; the pilot's proposed TEAM-01 name is mapped to the observed machine without treating its OS hostname or IP as authority.
- ✅ 2. Personal-computer and shared-computer rules coexist. Existing bindings and historical task traces remain readable without assigning a new human or host retrospectively.
- ✅ 3. The chosen paid provider/plan and current terms are checked against the proposed multi-person use. The outcome states exactly what is permitted, restricted or unresolved and does not infer a right from technical access.
- ✅ 4. A real common-computer scenario covers a personal HL publication and two distinct declared initiators in two projects, with isolated sessions/workspaces/access and inspectable durable launch/result lineage. Any unavailable client device is shown as unknown.
- ✅ 5. Independent review can reproduce the attribution and isolation claims from the recorded output; the owner sees the actual Plan passages and decides final acceptance.

## 6. Definition of Failure (DoF) 🔒 FROZEN

- ❌ 1. A host name, project binding, account display, OS user, title or self-declaration is presented as proof of a human identity or as a TFW mandate.
- ❌ 2. Shared use of a paid subscription is claimed permissible without checking the actual provider/plan terms, or a prohibited use is implemented as the accepted pilot.
- ❌ 3. Two projects share mutable working state, credentials or authority in a way that can attribute an act or result to the wrong project/person.
- ❌ 4. A migration rewrites historical initiators, breaks personal-machine use, adds a mandatory vendor runtime to Full TFW, or changes ATC's role routes by implication.
- ❌ 5. A diagram or simulated record is reported as a successful real-host trial.

**On failure:** stop the affected launch or claim, preserve the actual evidence, and return the smallest decision-changing issue through the task Coordinator to the owner. A provider restriction may require a new economic arrangement or a frozen-HL amendment; it is not waived by changing the transport.

## 7. Principles 🔒 FROZEN

1. **Separate facts by subject.** Person, declared principal, client device, execution host, provider account, working unit and mandate each have a different job. Neither naming nor colocation merges them.
2. **State confidence.** A person's self-declaration is useful provenance, but its weak assurance is explicit; an absent or conflicting declaration is unknown and supplies no authority.
3. **Keep authority local and exact.** The approved HL/TS, task-local status and immutable dispatch/selection lineage decide permission. Device identity is never a route, approval or grant.
4. **Preserve portability and history.** TFW rules live in inspectable project files; provider-specific connection details stay at their selected adapter or receiving environment. Do not rewrite old traces to suggest new knowledge.
5. **Prove the central claim.** A real host, two projects and the actual subscription terms are necessary evidence for the stated shared-computer objective; disclose limits rather than substitute a mockup.
6. **Record only material provenance.** Capture launches, authority-bearing transitions and durable writes needed for accountability; avoid a new all-actions telemetry system without demonstrated value.

### 7.1 Quality Contract 🔒 FROZEN

Across phases, use the same subject names and confidence meanings. A phase may refine storage and transport but may not turn declared identity into authentication, host name into authority, or subscription access into a license right.

### Risks of Not Researching

The work could encode an unusable subscription scheme, overstate who pressed a button, or select a single-host architecture that leaks authority across projects. Those findings would change acceptance and spending, not merely implementation details.

### Proposed RESEARCH Focus

1. Gather the actual host and provider/plan facts, official use terms, current TFW identity carriers and realistic remote-entry choices.
2. Compare the feasible combinations of host/session, human declaration, subscription account and project isolation.
3. Challenge the cheapest configuration against license restrictions, false attribution, parallel mutation and personal-machine compatibility.

### Why Not Just...?

| Approach | Value | Adoption / operating burden | Risks / tradeoffs | Decision and reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shared named host, separate project sessions, per-launch declared human and explicit assurance | Pools compute and aims to pool subscription while retaining honest attribution and parallel work | Moderate TFW contract and host setup; provider terms must be checked | Self-declaration is not authentication; one shared seat may be forbidden | Recommended for the owner's stated tolerance and target, conditional on research |
| Shared desktop/account with one persistent project binding and no per-launch declaration | Minimal setup and lowest immediate change | Low initial effort but high later investigation burden | Cannot tell which person started a task; parallel projects can be conflated | Reject for the stated attribution goal |
| Separate authenticated personal accounts or subscriptions on the same host | Stronger human attribution, clearer account separation | Higher setup and possibly license cost | May defeat one-subscription savings; provider capability still matters | Retain as fallback if H1 or H2 fails |

**Leave unchanged:** personal-machine bindings still work, but the proposed shared computer has no honest per-launch human attribution or tested parallel-project rule.

**Critical challenge:** The economic goal assumes a vendor allows several people to use one paid subscription on a shared computer. A technically working connection does not prove that assumption. If H1 fails, the task must surface the cost/goal conflict to the owner rather than fabricate compliant savings.
