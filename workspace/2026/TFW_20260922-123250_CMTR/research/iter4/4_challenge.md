## Findings

### C1 — The four-factor rule is not yet a decision rule

The same iteration-4 facts support opposite outcomes:

- **Sol + high:** cross-provider evidence, causal boundaries, and adversarial judgment raise uncertainty.
- **Sol + medium:** the task is bounded to one completed RES, current durable sources, no code/provider pilots, a reversible artifact, and a strong Coordinator gate.

Because iteration 3 names factors but no threshold, weight, hard constraint, or tie-breaker, either choice can be justified after the fact. The max-risk interpretation fails the economy goal: almost any intellectual task has at least one “high” factor and therefore drifts toward the strongest pair. Pure compensation also fails when it lets a strong oracle excuse missing capability, context, tools, or authority. Evidence-first escalation fails when the first error is irreversible or the oracle arrives too late.

A surviving rule shape therefore needs three distinct steps:

1. **Feasibility gate:** required context, tools, native availability, authority, and return route are hard constraints; effort cannot compensate for any missing item.
2. **Consequence/oracle fork:** when material failure is hard to reverse and the oracle is weak or late, choose conservatively; when failure is reversible and a strong timely oracle exists, an adjacent lower pair plus an explicit escalation trigger remains feasible.
3. **Epistemic label:** call the choice the lowest-resource **currently justified** sufficient candidate. Call it “minimum sufficient” only when comparative evidence covers the adjacent lower feasible candidate and economy is measured on the actual surface.

This preserves the HL quality floor while preventing a fluent rationale from becoming default-max.

### C2 — C5 can falsify a rationale; it cannot prove a universal minimum

Extract C5 is stronger than the current evidence, but four attacks survive:

1. **Nondeterminism:** the same prompt can yield different results. One Sol/high and one Sol/medium run cannot establish stable model/effort superiority.
2. **Not the same launch:** two fresh tasks differ in timing, source state, hidden backend state, and random generation. A committed packet reduces drift; it does not create the unobservable counterfactual of one exact launch.
3. **Readback/economy gaps:** on the current Codex task surface, effective model/effort and completed-task usage/quota are unavailable. C5 can compare requested pairs and artifacts, not applied-pair cost causally.
4. **Evaluation bias:** a Coordinator who knows which output used which requested pair may prefer the expected stronger result; output order and verbosity can bias automated or model judges.

OpenAI's evaluation guidance explicitly treats generative systems as variable, requires task-specific success criteria, and recommends pairwise/pass-fail comparisons. It also recommends randomized blinded human evaluation, clear scorecards and pass/fail thresholds, while warning about position and verbosity bias. Applied here, C5 should hide pair labels from the independent output judge while retaining them in the auditable dispatch trace; randomize output order; use the frozen oracle; and report the comparison as one bounded diagnostic.

[OpenAI evaluation best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices) · [OpenAI API deployment checklist](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/deployment-checklist)

C5 remains useful in two ways: a lower-pair pass can falsify a claim that high was demonstrably necessary for that packet, and a lower-pair material failure uniquely predicted by the rationale can support that boundary for that packet. Neither result proves a provider-wide or future-task minimum. Repetition or a representative corpus would strengthen generalization, but Phase B/Phase C need not create a permanent eval suite or claim one run is statistical proof.

### C3 — Iteration 4's own rework is a counterexample to “more reasoning fixes evidence”

The first Briefing return required two material corrections:

- H3/H4 statuses were copied from the re-frozen authority epoch rather than the newer free-section master HL visible in the Coordinator's current commit. That is a context/version-selection defect; more model capability or effort cannot recover a source that was not selected.
- The initial plan did not explicitly forbid treating iteration-3 Astra/high rule derivation and iteration-4 Sol/high RES attack as equivalent-work evidence. That is a reasoning/follow-through defect caught by the Coordinator's strong gate.

Sol + high therefore did not prevent all material evidence defects. This does **not** show that Sol + high was insufficient, that medium would have passed, or that a stronger model was needed. It shows why the rule must keep context, tools, oracle, and model/effort failure separate: the strong gate enabled cheap correction, while blind escalation would have misdiagnosed at least the source-version error.

The observed rework also weakens a “high because independent judgment” rationale that names no predicted failure: high still required correction, and the correction's causes divide across context and reasoning. The useful next-launch signal is failure type, not the mere existence of rework.

### C4 — The claim ladder survives only as a ceiling, not a mandatory progression

- **L1 intended** survives unchanged.
- **L2 applied** is desirable but may be unavailable. Its absence must cap attribution; it need not block an otherwise authorized launch.
- **L3 sufficient** must be phrased carefully when L2 is missing: “the delivered unit produced an accepted result under this oracle,” not “the requested pair proved sufficient.”
- **L4 minimum sufficient** cannot be a global factual claim from finite launch evidence. Untested candidates, nondeterminism, version drift, and unknown surface economy remain.

The defensible terminal claim is therefore one of:

- **provisional choice:** lowest-resource candidate currently justified by constraints and prior comparable evidence;
- **bounded comparative support:** adjacent lower candidate failed or passed this predeclared packet/oracle under stated limitations;
- **unknown:** no effective readback, comparable outcome, or surface economy supports attribution/minimum status.

This turns the ladder into a claim ceiling. It prevents missing readback from stopping useful work while preventing useful work from being misreported as proof of optimal selection.

| Prospective observation | What it supports | What it falsifies or weakens | What it does not prove |

| Requested and native readback differ, or substitution/clamp is undetected | adapter/readback defect | claim that dispatch applied the choice reliably | model quality of the requested pair |

Material rework is falsifying only when it breaches a predeclared quality-floor criterion and its cause is separated. Cosmetic edits, style preference, or a changed Coordinator expectation are not model-selection evidence.

### C7 — Surviving compact contract

Iteration 3's contract survives in narrowed form:

> For the exact next launch, first require the needed context, tools, native route, authority, and return. Then set the quality floor from uncertainty, dependencies, oracle timing/strength, and consequence/reversibility. Choose the lowest-resource pair currently justified on that surface; for the adjacent lower feasible pair, record either the predicted material failure that excludes it or that the choice is provisional. Record requested, effective when observable, delivery, outcome, and material rework separately. Correct the next launch by failure type. Claim minimum only from bounded comparative outcome and actual-surface economy; otherwise say unknown.

This is longer than the final core wording should be, but each clause removes a demonstrated ambiguity. Synthesis can compress it without dropping the feasibility gate, consequence/oracle fork, adjacent-lower discriminator, evidence separation, or epistemic label.
