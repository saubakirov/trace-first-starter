## Findings

### C1: Mixed product defect plus record typo does not drag unchanged items upward

Attack: one round contains wrong business behavior, an inaccurate RF count, and a stale label. The
VALUE item names the failed claim, oracle, harm, material consequence, Executor-owned completion and
Candidate change. The two TRACE items do not change acceptance or authority and receive finite
record-only repair/observation with `candidate_effect=preserved`. The aggregate verdict is `REVISE`
and the product item controls the executable act; neither record item becomes a TS/product reason.

Result: C1 holds. Current highest-authority sequencing is preserved, but item semantics prevent the
MFX failure where derivative-record cleanup appears to justify or enlarge execution.

### C2: Assurance-only failure preserves independently established VALUE

Attack: delivered behavior is independently verified against the right Candidate/environment, but a
required safety claim lacks an adequate oracle. The item is `ASSURANCE`, material because the safety
claim remains unestablished. It returns proof/correction work and names the missing oracle; it does
not claim the product is wrong or move Candidate unless correction actually changes VALUE.

Result: C1 holds. Approving would be a false approval; reimplementing unchanged behavior would be a
false return. The explicit `candidate_effect` and completion condition avoid both.

### C3: Low-frequency safety harm and reserved human acceptance cannot be waived

Attack A: unsafe-but-functional behavior has low estimated probability and every process record is
perfect. Attack B: evidence is sufficient but accepting the residual risk or external effect is
reserved to the owner. A generic materiality score could suppress A or let the Reviewer decide B.

C1 instead treats safety/security consequence and exact acceptance authority as protected
boundaries. A names severe harm and blocks on VALUE/ASSURANCE; B routes to the recorded ruler and
forbids Executor dispatch until that decision yields an executable bound. NASA's system-level
assurance/safety/security coordination supports the first boundary; the frozen RVAG mandate supplies
the second.

Result: C1 holds without granting the Reviewer business acceptance authority.

### C4: Sole versus redundant false evidence changes class, not by filename

Attack: the same stale evidence citation is evaluated twice. In the first case it is the only proof
for a material claim; in the second, an independent check establishes the same claim for the exact
subject/revision/environment/oracle tuple.

The first case is a material ASSURANCE gap; the second is a record-only TRACE defect. SLSA's
revision-bound identity example supports the identity half, while the independent proof determines
whether the claim remains established.

Result: C1 holds. A carrier-type rule would misclassify one case; E4 does not.

### C5: The strongest C4+ counterexample collapses into existing item routes

Attack: stakeholders need to know both “the result is acceptable” and “the record is unhealthy.” C4+
can display `VALUE=accept` and `RECORD=fail`.

C1 displays the same truth more precisely: `APPROVE` plus one or more visible TRACE observations or
finite record-only corrections, each with consequence and completion. If “record fail” blocks an
authorized action, it is material TRACE and must change the terminal verdict; if it does not, the
second verdict has no safe action beyond the existing disposition. No counterexample supplies
marginal decision power.

Result: C2/C4+ is eliminated. Its extra state risks being mistaken for human acceptance or a second
gate and violates subtraction-before-accretion.

### C6: New-carrier and non-code attacks remain expressible in existing files

Attack: a document has a materially false legal conclusion plus harmless citation formatting; an
operational runbook is correct but authorized by the wrong ruler; an analytical result is correct but
its sole dataset identity is unprovable. None is code, and all mix classes.

REVIEW already carries findings, verdict, disposition and traces; review stage files carry map,
verification and judgment; status/journal carry authorized state; the compact envelope signals the
Coordinator. E1 classifies each example without a score, registry, extra stage or provider schema.

Result: C1 and H6 hold across the non-code cases; C8 is eliminated.

### C7: Payload protocol attack requires semantic idempotence, not a second verdict

The exact envelope tuple
`{reviewer-unit, task-or-phase, verdict, review-artifact@immutable-ref}` is the logical message
identity. The Reviewer validates the current routing spine and resolves the artifact/ref before send.

| Attack | Required behavior | Forbidden consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong `coordinator_route` or mismatch with the activated spine | Refuse the send and any alternate recipient; preserve the durable verdict and route the control-record correction through existing authority before returning the same logical envelope | No owner/peer/GATEWAY fallback, no new verdict, no external effect |
| Stale or unresolved `REVIEW@ref`, or ref/verdict mismatch | Refuse the send; resolve the current durable REVIEW and immutable ref first | No guessed SHA, narrative substitute or second artifact |
| Duplicate delivery of byte-identical envelope | Treat it as the same logical signal keyed by the tuple; the Coordinator performs no second lifecycle/ruling act | No duplicate acceptance, dispatch, close or notification |
| Explicit failed send known not to have applied | Retry at most once with identical bytes and the same exact recipient after revalidating the spine/ref | No new REVIEW/status/journal/verdict and no prose expansion |
| Ambiguous outcome with no receipt/readback guarantee | Do not claim success or invent exactly-once support; retain an honest unavailable/blocked return condition under the existing durable artifact | No blind automatic retry or provider capability by analogy |
| Later genuine verdict/ref change | Treat it as a new bounded review result, not a retry of the old envelope | No mutation of the old logical identity |

[RFC 9110 §9.2.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-idempotent-methods) says an
automatic retry is safe only when the semantics are idempotent or the sender knows the original was
not applied, and warns against retrying a failed automatic retry. HTTP is not the TFW transport, but
the failure principle is directly useful. Google Pub/Sub's
[exactly-once documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/exactly-once-delivery)
shows that preventing duplicate processing depends on a stable message identity and observable
acknowledgment; without those, redelivery/duplicates remain possible. TFW therefore claims semantic
idempotence of the compact tuple, not universal exactly-once transport.

Result: the payload survives the requested attack. The protocol needs canonical workflow/convention
wording, not a new message registry or an edit to accepted AGSK transport.

### C8: The twelve-file denominator survives inclusion and exclusion attacks

**Included files are necessary:** Map owns material claim/risk selection; Verify owns evidence
identity, depth and test admission; Judge owns cross-row materiality and protected boundaries; REVIEW
owns item/aggregate output; the canonical workflow orders them and sends the envelope; conventions
own mixed-round lifecycle/routing; both config copies and Config Sync retire the ratio consistently;
glossary removes conflicting definitions; the two Claude command copies must remain byte-equivalent
to their full-copy sources.

**Excluded files have no demonstrated obligation:** the Codex and Antigravity Review skills are thin
routers; AGSK's rules/template/README already own compatible transport; manifest topology is
unchanged; Handoff already preserves accepted work/unaffected evidence and moves Candidate only on
VALUE; root coordination instructions already permit only the exact vertical edge. Editing any of
these would duplicate semantics rather than change behavior.

**Release split holds:** `VERSION`, CHANGELOG, the `3.5.1` guide and version fields are prepared only
after independent RVAG acceptance under the authorized release effect. Verification of unchanged
briefing/manifest/adapter topology does not make those paths implementation changes.

Result: no compatibility gap expands the twelve-file implementation denominator.

### C9: Ratio removal does not imply weak or arbitrary verification

Attack: without `0.42`, a Reviewer opens one convenient file and approves. E5 prevents this by
requiring a complete material-claim/risk/authority map, checks for every acceptance-critical claim,
affected dependency and oracle, and explicit unverified limits. A discovered discrepancy expands
only along a plausible failure mechanism or unresolved claim—not automatically across unrelated
files.

Attack: detection-power admission deletes useful green characterization tests. E6 admits them when a
targeted mutant, fault, external fixture or other negative control demonstrates relevant detection.

Result: H2/H4 survive; C5/C9 are eliminated without substituting another percentage or score.
