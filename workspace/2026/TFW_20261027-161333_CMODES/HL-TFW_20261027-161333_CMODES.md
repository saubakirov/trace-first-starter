## 1. Vision 🔒 FROZEN

TFW offers three understandable ways to organize the same governed work: manual operation, agents in visible chats, and one Coordinator operating through native subagents. Each mode supports both single-phase and multi-phase tasks. The selected mode carries authorized work through research, execution, independent review, corrections and close while preserving the owner's decisions and a result that another authorized participant can continue.

**Impact:** Owners choose how they want to interact once; Coordinators do not reject legitimate subagents or demand additional visible phase chats merely because a task has several phases. Consuming projects receive one portable semantic contract with truthful platform-specific capabilities.

## 3. Target State (To-Be) 🔒 FROZEN

One common coordination contract defines three owner-facing modes. Their labels describe who starts roles and where those roles live; mode selection does not replace activation authority, communication policy, approval boundaries or role ownership.

| Mode | Launcher and working units | Owner experience | Single / multiple phases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manual | The owner starts the required distinct role sessions. Native returns may remain available; fully manual transfer is an explicit selection. | The owner controls launches and receives clear next actions. | Both admitted. |
| Visible agents | The authorized Coordinator creates and advances directly addressable visible role chats. The existing bounded phase-Coordinator arrangement remains available for long work. | Role chats are visible; routine authorized handoffs do not require copied commands. | Both admitted. |
| Subagents | One owner-facing Coordinator creates native subagents for Researcher, Executor and independent Reviewer, and owns readiness, phase planning and continuation across the whole task. | One main Coordinator chat; subagents may appear in native tool views but no separate user-owned role chats are required. | Both admitted; additional phase Coordinators are not mandatory. |

The common rule applies to Codex, Claude Code and other adapters wherever the selected surface supplies the required mechanisms. Each adapter explains its actual route and any precise missing capability. A generic provider label is neither permission nor proof; a platform limitation must not recreate a methodology-wide ban or silently substitute a different selected mode.

In subagent mode, phases retain their own bounded scope, dependencies, authoritative state and role artifacts. The same Coordinator may derive each ready phase's HL/TS and manage its workers. It receives bounded durable results, advances ready work and reuses the Executor and independent Reviewer for corrections where the native surface permits it. Slots, lifetime, interruption and restart are handled explicitly; task phase count is not equated with simultaneous worker count.

Independent review uses a fixed reachable Candidate and an independent role context. The Coordinator retains its Role Lock and never writes RES, ONB, RF, REVIEW or implementation merely because workers are subagents. Durable task files support continuation when a worker or Coordinator context is replaced; identity, source bounds, unfinished work and authority are revalidated rather than inferred from inherited conversation.

The three modes preserve model/effort selection under actual controls, exact activation and return routes, owner reservations, safe mode changes, trace ownership and completion obligations. Completion means the accepted product, required documentation/knowledge/changelog effects and truthful resource coverage; it is not a successful launch or a subagent saying “done.”
