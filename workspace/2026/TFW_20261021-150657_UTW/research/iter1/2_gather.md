## Findings

### G1 — Understanding is an evidential claim about a person in a situation

[S1: Rozenblit and Keil, 2002](https://cogdevlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/rozenblit%20%26%20keil%20%202002.pdf), introduction and Studies 1–2: explanatory self-ratings decrease after people attempt explanations. The study concerns calibration of knowledge about devices, not validation of a managerial assessment. It challenges treating confidence as understanding; it does not show that every explanation prompt improves competence.

[S2: Chi et al., 1989](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1), publisher abstract: successful mechanics learners generated explanations connecting example actions to principles and relied less on examples later. This is a comparison of learners and their protocols, not a randomized TFW intervention. The [author-hosted scan](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chibassoklewisreimannglaser_1.pdf) was accessible but yielded no readable text through the current extraction; no full-method assessment is claimed.

**Researcher inference:** an operational claim should identify person/responsibility, question, conditions, expected answer grounds, observed reasoning/errors and permitted inference. Recognizing a correct option, explaining a practiced example and predicting a novel consequence can each be useful, but are not interchangeable. A supported decision can satisfy a real managerial need; the material must not silently claim unaided mastery. H1 remains plausible but unproved for TFW.

### G2 — Representation can change the effort of inference without increasing knowledge

[S3: Larkin and Simon, 1987](https://mechanism.ucsd.edu/bill/teaching/F12/cs200/Readings/larkin.whyadiagramissometimesworth.1987.pdf), sections 1 and conclusion: the analysis distinguishes equivalent information from equivalent computational effort. Spatial grouping may reduce search or expose useful relations. Benefits depend on the representation and the operations the solver knows; an arbitrary diagram is not guaranteed to help. These are computational analyses of mathematics/physics examples, not a trial of managers or AI-generated maps.

**Implication:** compare forms using equivalent decision-relevant content. Otherwise better results may reflect added information rather than the diagram. Include reading/interaction effort and omissions. A diagram's practical benefit can be real even if it chiefly supports external reasoning; that is a bounded product outcome, not proof of internal learning. This counters both mandatory-diagram and diagram-is-merely-decoration assumptions.

### G3 — Goals focus action; the focus itself needs examination

[S4: Locke and Latham, 2002](https://med.stanford.edu/content/dam/sm/s-spire/documents/PD.locke-and-latham-retrospective_Paper.pdf), Core Findings, Task Complexity and New Directions: the theory's authors synthesize evidence for specific challenging goals, with conditions including ability, commitment, feedback and task strategy. They discuss learning versus performance goals and conflict between local and organizational goals. Thus the positive literature itself does not license a universal performance target for unfamiliar work.

The [Ordonez et al. 2009 author-institution abstract](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goals-gone-wild-systematic-side-effects-overprescribing-goal-setting), previously inspected in Briefing, supplies a counter-position; its full-text retrieval remains unavailable, so no detailed side-effect magnitude is adopted. The attempted AOM rebuttal retrieval also failed; the dispute is not adjudicated here.

**Implication:** separate the chosen outcome, the strategy expected to reach it, the local target and the evidence being collected. A useful exploration task may aim to learn which strategy works. Missing results can mean delayed observation or failed assumptions; it is not automatically misconduct or drift. These distinctions constrain H4's proposed signal.

### G4 — Contribution requires more than a chain of arrows

[S5: Mayne, 2012](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Mayne12-CAComingofAge.pdf), pp. 271–274 and Table 1: contribution analysis investigates a theory of change using observed results, assumptions and other influencing factors. The account includes risks, unintended effects and rival explanations. It distinguishes contribution from assigning a quantitative share of an outcome to one intervention. It is a methodological argument for evaluation, not evidence that a short TFW contribution statement improves decisions.

**Researcher inference:** use two different claim levels when comparing options:

```text
Before outcome evidence: work -> expected mechanism -> intended outcome -> purpose
                                  assumptions / possible defeats
After observation:       what happened + competing explanations -> bounded contribution claim
```

The arrows express a proposal until supported. At a material change, ask whether an assumption changed, whether the expected link still has grounds, and who may decide a new direction. A goal's legitimacy remains an authority question. This provides a candidate reasoning form; it does not select a new template, registry, score or recurring gate.

### G6 — Existing software forms demonstrate representability, not complete extraction

[S6: C4 system-context guidance](https://c4model.com/diagrams/system-context) defines a coarse view around people and interacting systems; its audience includes nontechnical readers. That is a useful scope pattern, not an empirical comprehension result or a domain-neutral TFW requirement.

[S7: Structurizr DSL](https://docs.structurizr.com/dsl) and its [minimal example](https://docs.structurizr.com/dsl/example) demonstrate a textual architecture model and a view rendered from declared entities/relations. The example establishes a model-to-view path. It does not establish automatic discovery of true business meaning, source completeness or semantic currentness. No installation or execution was performed. Cookbook pages were opened but no uninspected behavior is relied upon.

[S8: Nygard's original ADR account, 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions) uses short decision/context/consequence records and preserves superseded decisions. Its early favorable experience is practitioner testimony, not a controlled cost or comprehension comparison. TFW already stores comparable decision grounds, so this does not justify a second ADR collection.

**Implication:** H3 has plausible existing constituents, but their composition and domain portability remain untested. A representation format, an evidence extractor and an authoritative decision record solve different problems.

### G7 — Fresh rendering does not establish semantic currency; H2 lacks cost evidence

Local `docs/scripts/gen_docs.py`, `validate_sources`/`copy_page` at the same epoch, checks required source existence, reads Markdown, rewrites links, adds frontmatter and writes output. This bounded static inspection supports a source-to-render probe. It does not establish end-to-end execution or all generator behavior. No run or code change occurred.

**Constructed counterexample, not an observed defect:** an unchanged eligibility document can become wrong after an external policy change; re-rendering the same bytes will preserve that error. Conversely, punctuation edits can change bytes without changing the relevant decision. Therefore source change detection and semantic applicability need separate interpretations.

H2's economic comparison remains open. No inspected source quantifies TFW setup/maintenance savings. Extract should expose these cost drivers without fictional numbers:

| Driver | What changes the burden | Needed pilot observation |

| Semantic maintenance | Changed assumptions, external dependencies, abstraction repairs | Work required for a selected material change |
| Extraction and rendering | Stack/tool setup, coverage failures, repeat generation | Native usage and tool execution, separated from curation |
| Human use | Reading, clarification, scenario difficulty, error correction | Time/assistance and correctness recorded separately |
| Review and opportunity cost | Displaced work and decisions improved or delayed | Specific decision benefit and simplify/omit rationale |

These are measurement categories, not a tariff, effort estimate or approved budget.
