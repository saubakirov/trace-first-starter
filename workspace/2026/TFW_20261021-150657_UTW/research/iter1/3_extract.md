### E3 — A question is both a measurement opportunity and a possible intervention

Primary evidence added in this stage, inspected 2026-10-21:

- [Karpicke and Blunt, 2011, author-hosted Science paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf), methods/results for both experiments: retrieval practice outperformed the studied concept-mapping condition on delayed questions including inference. The conditions differed in material availability and learning activity; this is not a clean estimate of diagram format alone. It concerns educational texts, not TFW managerial decisions.
- [Blunt and Karpicke, 2014, author-hosted Journal of Educational Psychology paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Blunt_Karpicke_JEDP.pdf), abstract and Experiment 1 methods/results: retrieval using maps and paragraphs produced similar delayed performance in that study. The abstract reports the second experiment crossing format and text availability. This directly counters the interpretation that the earlier result establishes an intrinsically inferior visual format. Educational-text results do not prove a workplace intervention.
- [Mislevy, Almond and Lukas, 2003, ETS report identity](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2003/hsgs.html), with the [publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.2333-8504.2003.tb01908.x) inspected earlier in this stage: evidence-centered design frames assessment through arguments connecting performance to inferences about knowledge. Full report retrieval failed (including the mirror), and the later publisher retry returned 403. No detailed psychometric method or validated TFW rubric is attributed to this abstract.

**Researcher inference:** fix the intended claim and answer grounds before a pilot response, then state the limit of the inference. This lightweight argument does not require adopting a test platform, psychometric model or recurring exam:

| Intended bounded claim | Observable response | Grounds and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Person can use available material for this decision | Explains the relevant path, predicts a changed consequence and identifies a material unknown | Check against source-grounded relations and plausible alternatives; supports this assisted or view-supported case only |
| Person retains relevant reasoning without the material | Separately observed response with disclosed material withdrawal and prior exposure | Different claim and burden; no inference from a view-supported answer alone |
| Work is expected to serve its purpose | Names the mechanism, material assumption and what would defeat it | Proposal evaluated against approved purpose; not observed contribution |
| An observed outcome supports a contribution claim | Connects observations to the proposed mechanism while considering rivals | Bounded claim with missing evidence explicit; neither attribution percentage nor automatic alignment |

Recognition can check a specific distinction; recall can check availability of facts; neither alone demonstrates consequence reasoning. Fluent language is not a scoring criterion. A useful response may correctly refuse a conclusion because a causal link is unknown. Expected relations/acceptable alternatives should be grounded independently of the generated explanation; simply asking its authoring agent to score itself risks shared error. Later TS must decide a proportionate human/reviewer judgment, not assume validation here.

Pre/post repetition is not a passive measurement: earlier questions, correction and source access may change later performance. Record initial response, actual clarification and later response separately when needed for the claim; use a distinct relevant case if transfer is claimed. Do not turn this methodological caution into a mandatory unaided examination.

### E4 — Strategic scrutiny can be an added question inside the existing Purpose Check

Gather G4/G5 established both the contribution distinction and the substantial current baseline. Across C1–C4, the candidate addition is conditional: when the work-to-outcome connection is consequential and uncertain, expose the particular assumption and counter-observation that could change the decision. This can live in existing decision grounds. It is not a new goal score or approval gate.

Constructed TFW comparison: an author adds a detailed view to improve understanding. Existing Purpose Check can already ask which baseline/North Star clause it serves, whether it is excess, and what harm omission causes. The incremental contribution question is: “Through what expected use will this view improve the responsible person's decision, and what observation would make us simplify it?” Equal or better reasoning with the concise baseline, or maintenance consuming the expected benefit, would challenge that proposed mechanism. Neither has been observed here.

Changed assumptions, absent observations and authorized strategy changes need different dispositions. A changed assumption warrants reconsideration; absent evidence warrants uncertainty; an authorized change warrants comparison with the new effective authority. None automatically proves drift. Whether this question yields more useful decisions than the existing Purpose Check is still H4's empirical gap.
