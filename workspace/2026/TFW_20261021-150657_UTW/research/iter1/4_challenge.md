## Findings

### CH1 — More scrutiny can help while making support harder to use

New primary sources inspected on 2026-10-21:

- [Koedinger and Aleven, 2007, author-hosted published paper](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/paper%20%2314.pdf), abstract and framework/Tables 1–2: the authors examine assistance through their Cognitive Tutor research and describe costs on both sides of giving/withholding information, including confusion and wasted effort. They treat the optimal balance as conditional and unresolved. This is educational tutoring evidence, not validation of C3 or proof that every user needs the same orientation.
- [Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos, 2021, original manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.09692v1), abstract, introduction and sections 3.1–3.3: their controlled nutrition-choice task used simulated AI and compared explanation conditions with interventions such as initial independent choice or delayed advice. They report reduced overreliance with cognitive forcing, alongside worse subjective acceptance for the most effective designs and unequal benefits across cognitive motivation. This is not a generative-agent workplace trial and does not justify a compulsory examination.
- [Vasconcelos et al., 2023, original manuscript v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06823v2), abstract, Study 1 summary and sections 10.3–10.4: five maze-task studies examine task/explanation cost and incentives. They find conditions in which explanations reduce overreliance, countering an unconditional claim that explanations cannot help. The authors explicitly limit generalization to uncertain/open-ended work and note their idealized explanation quality. Appendix 12.3 discloses earlier question-answering studies stopped early; no effect estimate from those is used here.

**Inference for this task:** treat accuracy, effort and acceptability as separate observations, and make verification easier before assuming more imposed effort is better. The sources do not validate a universal task order, comprehension score or TFW benefit. The owner-authorized gate independently requires explanation access without a correct-answer prerequisite. C3* therefore offers “show me the whole first” and permits stopping the scenario; needing orientation is not a personnel failure. The existing actual-human pilot obligation remains, but support is not withheld to enforce it.
