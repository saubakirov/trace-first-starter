# Reading an AI economics report

Use these questions before making a decision from the report.

1. **What result was achieved?** Find the intended value, delivered result and actual acceptance or release state. An expenditure total alone does not prove value.
2. **Which facts count once?** Check projects, tasks, units and source ranges. Repeated receipts, superseded snapshots and parent summaries must not add the same consumption again.
3. **What does the product label mean?** Separate resources associated with a task from resources allocated to a feature. Overlapping keywords select a unique task union; they are not additive budget buckets.
4. **Which time question is answered?** Distinguish consumption in a period from the lifetime cost of tasks created or completed in that period. Retain undated consumption in lifetime totals and show it separately.
5. **What do tokens, prices and time measure?** Inspect token categories, the dated rate card, currency and unpriced models. API-equivalent prices do not establish subscription bills; collector time does not establish whole-agent overhead.
6. **Where does coverage end?** Read exact units, bounds, cutoffs, exclusions, missing bindings, failures and the last good snapshot. A complete selected prefix does not prove completed work; unknown values are not zero.
7. **Which conclusions are supported?** Separate observations, interpretations and causal hypotheses. Ask for the comparison baseline before accepting an anomaly or a claim of savings, efficiency or ROI.
8. **What is the next grounded action?** Choose a specific check or workflow improvement, cite its evidence and identify who has authority to decide. Preserve uncertainty that the next check must resolve.

Record the decision, supporting sources and unresolved question together so the next reader can reproduce it.
