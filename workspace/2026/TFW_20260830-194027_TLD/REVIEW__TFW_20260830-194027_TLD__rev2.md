## 4. Verdict

**✅ APPROVE**

All ten judge rows hold. The finding that grounded the first verdict is closed at the source, at the
consumer, and structurally — the search runs from the shipped adapter copy with byte-identical output, and
the hazard is written into the canon so the next author meets it rather than rediscovers it.

Three things about the revisions are worth stating rather than absorbing silently:

1. **The executor found the trap I had not.** My fix instruction said to state the hazard where the next
   author will hit it. The obvious place was `review.md` — and `review.md` is itself a substituted copy, so
   the warning would have arrived mangled inside the file warning about it. Deleting the note rather than
   rewording it was also right: the rule already stands in two places, and a third copy in an over-budget
   file is maintenance.
2. **Revision 2 corrected its own observation by a factor of 430 and did the harder thing.** O1 called the
   frontmatter leak cosmetic and put it on two pages; it was 860 of 990, from three independent causes, one
   of which is structurally invisible to the POSIX CI that gates merges. Each cause was removed rather than
   guarded, and the guard for the invisible one was deliberately written at source level after an
   end-to-end scanner was built and thrown away for being vacuously green on Linux. Two pre-existing
   assertions that checked the *quoting* rather than the parsed value — and would have passed against all
   three defects — were replaced.
3. **Both revisions kept the historical record intact.** RF §1–§9 unchanged, stale figures left as written
   with the correction beside them, the screenshot unedited, my REVIEW and stage files untouched. The
   discipline the task is about was applied to the task's own trace.

The one thing I could not verify from the tree — the owner's ruling converting O1 from a task to a payment
— I asked about instead of assuming, and it is confirmed. That question is the whole content of the
existence test, met from the reviewer's chair.
