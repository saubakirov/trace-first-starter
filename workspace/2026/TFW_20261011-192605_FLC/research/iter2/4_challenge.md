### C2 — Correct capture in K4 does not prove complete memory

The source-first K4 probe reduces the bias of starting only from known published records: an eligible role-produced technical distinction was selected before its downstream outcome. Its RF → REVIEW → selected technical-reference/index path is observable. It defeats the claim that an empty human Fact Candidates section or absent standalone record necessarily means lost technical knowledge.

But one selected retained distinction cannot reveal insights omitted from the RF altogether. K3 deliberately tests a non-publication control, so it cannot be counted as a successful capture of human knowledge. K1/K2 are known public decisions with unusually explicit sources. These selection properties preclude a capture percentage and general completeness claim. No missing insight is invented to balance the sample.

Conversely, K2's broader later qualification does not establish prior noncompliance: its earlier selection had a narrower stated purpose. The visible repair/extension followed an owner request and actual confirmation. It demonstrates a human route and a coverage dependency, while leaving unknown whether an already-owed earlier insight was missed. The retired batch counter cannot detect content that was never preserved; restoring it alone has no demonstrated causal remedy in this sample.

### C3 — The hybrid is historically feasible, not proved superior

C1's explicit selection and returned independent work survive the attack that the hybrid is merely an imagined topology. Distinct C1/B2 material review findings also contradict the claim that subagents necessarily reduce review to a label. These are bounded documentary observations, not a guarantee of independent judgment in every run.

The favorable explanation that the transport switch caused success does not survive as a causal conclusion: owner changes, prerequisite resolution, delivery order and cumulative work changed too. The unfavorable explanation that more hierarchy necessarily causes noncompletion also exceeds the evidence: the same broad arrangement reaches a finite result in C1. Neither observation establishes the benefit of the extra task/phase boundary or a clean direct-single-Coordinator comparison.

Available artifacts preserve recovery inputs and same-role continuations; no induced crash/context-loss recovery was observed. Privacy depends on actual source/return boundaries, not fewer role addresses. Owner attention and traffic are not quantified. C1's mixed four-source phase total and B2's single-Reviewer receipt therefore remain descriptive, unequal measurements; no ranking follows even if one raw number is lower.

### C4 — Human authority survives the mechanism change; usability remains a real question

The current migration explicitly replaces numeric planning bookkeeping with material handover/use/close functions, installs compatible readers before retiring the old live settings, and preserves old state/markers as historical evidence. It does not authorize new records merely because an update occurred. The current workflow still requires an actual selected human decision or valid grant, as the K2 source chain concretely illustrates.

This rebuts the broad claim that human qualification was abolished. It does not rebut the owner's difficulty understanding where the decision now occurs. The route is less visibly tied to a global count and instead depends on a source-specific selection and disposition. A clear explanation of that existing route can be warranted without claiming a code defect, adding a new mandatory approval, or restoring an unproven counter-based remedy.

The remaining installation caveat is scoped: compatible-reader ordering is a requirement, not evidence that every receiver adopted it correctly. This pass establishes no migration failure, universal compliance or general knowledge-use reliability. H6/H7 cannot be globally resolved from four probes.
