## 1. Vision 🔒 FROZEN

Every terminal TFW task, including an unsuccessful one, now carries a proportionate, inspectable account of the tokens, time, quality observations, and money attributable to it, with each number tied to its source and calculation method. Each task also carries 3–5 keywords that identify the relevant product domains and features. The same evidence supports careful comparison across product areas, teams, TFW releases, and coordination modes, so the company can improve practice and resource allocation without rewarding cheap but ineffective work.

**Impact:** An owner can see the economics of one task and the part of the product it served; a finance or product lead can group comparable work by domain or feature, distinguish measured consumption from allocated subscription cost, and identify practices worth sharing. The unit of analysis is the task and product area, not an individual worker.

> **Imagined stakeholder quote:** “For comparable work, this team reduced elapsed time without increasing revisions; here are the task reports and the limits of the comparison.”

The **capture unit** is a task. The **financial decision unit** is a product domain or feature across tasks. The **practice-learning unit** is a comparable cohort of tasks. Moving between these units requires an explicit selection and allocation rule; a keyword search is not an accounting ledger.

| Measure family | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|
| Tokens | Provider-observed input/output usage bound to the task and role; unavailable data stays unknown. |
| Time | Agent active time, human contribution time when voluntarily supplied by role, and calendar lead time are separate. Waiting between events is not counted as a person's working time. |
| Money | Actual variable bills, allocated fixed subscriptions or shared resources, and optional standard-rate valuation of human time are separate cost pools; a pool is counted once. |
| Quality | Purpose acceptance, independent review, material defects and revisions are shown beside resource use; rejected tasks remain visible. |
| Product location | 3–5 domain/feature keywords identify the work; one primary feature or explicit shared allocation supports additive financial totals. |

Six months after adoption, a task closes and the owner sees this one-page report. The Full example below uses illustrative values, **not measurements**; an Assisted or Daily task shows inapplicable roles as `N/A` and still carries the same keywords, cost distinctions, and coverage:

```text
Product: TFW                              TFW start/close: 3.6.1 / 3.6.1
Keywords (4): domain:methodology · feature:coordination
              feature:task-report · feature:cost-attribution
Primary cost feature: coordination (one additive attribution)
Mode: separate role tasks                   Quality: purpose accepted; AC 8/8

Role             Tokens in   Tokens out   Active time   Cost basis
Coordinator         31,000       11,000        1h 10m    subscription; role $ unknown
Researcher          52,000       16,000        2h 05m    subscription; role $ unknown
Executor            89,000       36,000        3h 40m    subscription; role $ unknown
Reviewer            22,000        9,000        0h 55m    subscription; role $ unknown
Total              194,000       72,000        7h 50m    task bill unknown

Calendar elapsed: 2d 02h       Approval wait: 18h 20m (task event interval)

Coverage: tokens 4/4 roles; active time 4/4; role-level dollars unknown

```

At planning, the owner can type these keywords directly, or accept/edit a Coordinator proposal in the existing task brief. The report repeats the selected 3–5 terms so the product connection is visible without opening another file. Keywords support search and overlapping filters; **they do not silently split cost**. The Coordinator's proposed default assigns the whole ordinary task to one primary feature for additive totals; genuinely shared work uses `feature:shared`. A percentage split is exceptional and needs an explicit, grounded rule whose shares sum to 100%.

The comparison is equally explicit about its denominator. Here the selected filter is `feature:coordination`, with no person-level view:

| Comparable cohort (illustrative) | Tasks | Accepted / rejected | Cohort cost / accepted result | Median active time | Median tokens | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Manual coordination, same task class | 8 | 6 / 2 | $96 / 6 = $16 | 9.1 h | 281k | 8/8 time/cost; 6/8 tokens |
| Separate role tasks, same task class | 7 | 6 / 1 | $84 / 6 = $14 | 7.8 h | 266k | 7/7 time/cost/tokens |

This view permits a question about practice; it does not by itself establish that the coordination mode caused the difference. A useful comparison links the selected task traces and names the practice that might transfer, not only a cheaper cohort. Phase A establishes the Full task account, Phase B adapts the same meaning to lighter task paths, and Phase C supplies the comparison and its interpretation guardrails.

## 6. Definition of Failure (DoF) 🔒 FROZEN

- ❌ 1. An inferred or allocated dollar amount is presented as an actual provider bill or exact marginal task cost.
- ❌ 2. Missing token/time observations are filled with invented zeros or silent estimates, or a number cannot be traced to a method and source epoch.
- ❌ 3. A team or coordination mode is ranked from raw spend or speed without task comparability, quality, revision, and coverage context.
- ❌ 4. The report becomes a second authoritative lifecycle state, a raw conversation archive, or a mandatory cross-task mutable index.
- ❌ 5. Instrumentation requires private reasoning, credentials, unrelated chat inspection, or unsupported provider behavior.
- ❌ 6. Provider-level token capture is unavailable in every investigated route, yet the product claims measured per-task token economics without an owner-approved change of scope.
- ❌ 7. Keywords are absent, name people instead of product domains/features, or multiply one task's cost across several features without a stated allocation rule.
- ❌ 8. Task and team economics are repurposed into individual monitoring or a per-person efficiency ranking.
- ❌ 9. Failed or rejected tasks disappear from cost denominators, making successful work appear artificially cheap.
- ❌ 10. A lighter task path is reported as covered by copying Full role rows or claiming measurements that its workflow cannot produce.

**On failure:** Preserve the evidence, state the unsupported claim or missing source, return to the owner for a bounded scope decision, and revise the approved contract through §12 if its frozen outcome must change.

## 7. Principles 🔒 FROZEN

1. **Measure the decision, not activity for its own sake.** Every collected field must support a task-close or management decision that the owner named.
2. **Provenance before precision.** Distinguish measured, derived, allocated, estimated, and unknown values; record source epoch and method for every numeric conclusion.
3. **Quality and purpose alongside consumption.** Savings are beneficial only when the accepted result still serves its purpose and its evidence is adequate.
4. **Comparable cohorts before conclusions.** Control or disclose task type, size/complexity, quality, and missingness before comparing teams, releases, or modes; do not claim causation from a simple before/after table.
5. **Task-local truth, portable comparison.** Keep authoritative records with their tasks and derive cross-task views on demand from inspectable, provider-independent sources.
6. **Proportionate capture.** Prefer existing durable task and provider observations; do not add reporting ceremony whose cost defeats the information value.
7. **Product keywords without surveillance.** Use 3–5 domain/feature keywords to locate where product resources went; workflow roles explain cost composition, while people are not the unit of measurement.

### 7.1 Quality Contract 🔒 FROZEN

All phases preserve the distinction between accepted purpose, verified quality, cost, and time. Reports show data coverage and do not compress these into a single team score. Team comparisons are aggregate practice comparisons; no per-person productivity score or monitoring view is accepted. A provider or task-path integration may have narrower coverage than the provider-neutral report contract and must state that boundary.

## 10. RESEARCH Case 🟢 FREE

### Blind Spots

- Which current Codex and other target surfaces expose task- and role-level token counts, timestamps, effective model settings, and usage records with stable identifiers, including lighter task paths?
- How should fixed subscriptions, API billing, currency conversion, and shared hardware be kept separate in an honest task report?
- Can 3–5 owner-correctable domain/feature keywords remain meaningful across projects and releases without a burdensome central registry?
- Which financial allocation rule gives useful product-part totals without multiplying shared-task costs or creating false precision?

### Hypotheses

| # | Decision-changing hypothesis | Plausible false case and consequence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | At least one target provider exposes measured task/role token usage that can be bound to a TFW task. | Only a shared quota or incomplete conversation usage is visible; the token capture outcome and first supported route need owner redecision. | Proposed for owner review |
| H2 | Time and monetary bases can be reported without treating calendar waiting or subscription allocation as provider processing time or actual task billing. | Timestamps or price basis are missing; affected fields remain unknown and comparisons narrow. | Proposed for owner review |
| H3 | Owners can supply or correct 3–5 stable domain/feature keywords within the ordinary task brief, and a separate allocation rule can handle shared work. | Terms drift across teams or multi-feature work dominates; keyword normalization, a shared category, or a different allocation unit becomes necessary. | Proposed for owner review |
| H4 | There is enough purpose, quality, rework, and task-context evidence, including rejected work, to compare teams, releases, and modes without judging individuals. | Task mix, survivorship bias, small cohorts, or missing outcomes dominate; publish bounded case studies or restrict views rather than rankings or causal gains. | Proposed for owner review |
| H5 | Full, Assisted, and optional Daily can share the same measurement meanings without sharing the same reporting ceremony. | Lighter paths lack durable role or terminal signals; the report must use N/A/unknown and a smaller form, or coverage must be narrowed by an explicit owner decision. | Coordinator-selected for research |

### Risks of Not Researching

A mandatory close report could promise exact tokens or dollars that the provider cannot expose, while aggregate comparisons could favor teams with easier tasks or weaker review.

### Proposed RESEARCH Focus

1. **Gather:** Map actual usage, timing, billing, role-identity, and task-linking sources in the chosen pilot surface; distinguish documented capability from a live observation.
2. **Extract:** Define reproducible measures, source epochs, missingness, cost categories, and a minimal product/feature classification.
3. **Challenge:** Try false attribution, mixed subscriptions, multi-feature work, incomparable tasks, and mode changes; state which conclusions survive.

### Decision alternatives to test

| Product-part accounting choice | Implementation and operating cost | Consequence if chosen or rejected |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5 keywords only | Lowest entry burden; no allocation decision | Useful search/filtering, but no additive financial total by feature. Rejecting it adds an accounting choice to every task. |
| One primary feature plus 3–5 descriptive keywords; shared category or explicit split only when material | Small ordinary-task burden; exceptional shared tasks need a reasoned choice | **Coordinator-selected draft approach:** additive totals stay possible with visible coarseness. Rejecting it may force either no totals or routine percentage estimates. |
| Percentage split among all named features | Highest capture and review burden; apparent precision may exceed evidence | Supports detailed totals if shares are grounded. Rejecting it loses that detail but avoids arbitrary percentages on ordinary tasks. |

The owner delegated this design choice to the Coordinator. The middle option is the draft decision because it yields additive financial totals with limited routine effort; research may disprove its usefulness before HL approval. If the split cannot be grounded, `feature:shared` is more honest than a percentage invented for accounting completeness.

### Why Not Just...?

- Why not count text in chat files? — Chat text is not provider token accounting and would pressure TFW toward transcript storage.
- Why not divide the monthly subscription by task count? — The quotient is an allocation policy, not observed marginal cost; it may still be useful if labeled and justified.
- Why not publish a team efficiency leaderboard? — Raw cost and time omit task difficulty, purpose, quality, and missing data.
